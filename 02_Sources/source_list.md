# Sources — AI Regulatory Comparison Brief

Primary legal texts prioritized where accessible; secondary legal-tracking sources used where a
primary text isn't practically citable (e.g., a bill tracker's plain-English summary of a statute).

## European Union
1. **Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 (EU AI Act).** Official Journal of the European Union.
   Primary source for the Act's original structure and staged-application dates.
2. **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744 (Digital Omnibus on AI).** Official Journal of the European Union,
   published 24 July 2026, in force 27 July 2026.
   Primary source for the Annex III/Annex I deferrals and the Article 50(2) transitional.

## United States
3. **Texas HB 149 (Texas Responsible Artificial Intelligence Governance Act / TRAIGA).** Signed
   22 June 2025, effective 1 January 2026.
   Primary legislative text; secondary confirmation via multiple law-firm client alerts
   (Baker Botts, K&L Gates, Nelson Mullins) cross-checked for consistency.
4. **California SB 53 (Transparency in Frontier Artificial Intelligence Act)** and **AB 2013**,
   effective 1 January 2026; **California Civil Rights Council ADMT regulations.**
   Primary/secondary mix; scope and applicability confirmed against multiple independent trackers.
5. **Colorado SB 24-205** (repealed) and **SB 26-189** (Automated Decision-Making Technology Act),
   signed 14 May 2026, effective 1 January 2027.
   Secondary sources (multiple independent legal-tracking sites, cross-checked against each other
   given the primary bill text's complexity and the fast-moving litigation context) used for the
   enforcement-stay and DOJ-intervention narrative specifically, since that sequence of events is
   not itself codified in the statutory text.

## India
6. **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, Government of India. India AI Governance
   Guidelines, published 5 November 2025, under the IndiaAI Mission.**
   Primary source (PIB press release and MeitY guideline document) for the seven sutras, the
   institutional structure (AIGG, TPEC, AISI), and the policy rationale for not enacting a
   standalone AI statute. This source is also what corrected the "Do No Harm" error — see
   `research_log.md`.
7. **Ministry of Electronics and Information Technology, Government of India. Digital Personal
   Data Protection Act, 2023, and DPDP Rules, 2025** (staged commencement) — see Project 2 of
   this portfolio for full citation and verification detail.

## Note on source hierarchy
Where a primary legal text (an Official Journal publication, a signed bill) was directly
identifiable, it is cited as the primary source. For the Colorado litigation narrative
specifically, no single primary court filing was practical to cite for a portfolio piece, so
multiple independent secondary legal-tracking sources were cross-checked against each other for
consistency before the facts were treated as reliable enough to include.
